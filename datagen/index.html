Case study · Datagen · 2021–2023

Synthetic data for computer vision, in a few clicks instead of months.

Datagen generates human-centric synthetic data for training computer vision models. I led design of its self-serve platform: the place where engineers browse, tweak and order datasets, and the bridge to the SDK they use for more control.

Product Design Lead

Worked with
Product management, R&D and the 3D team
Users
Computer vision and machine learning engineers
01 / The problem

Getting good training data took months.

Computer vision engineers need clean, labeled, secure and legal datasets. Collecting and labeling that data by hand is slow and expensive, and can take months or even years. Datagen’s answer was a self-serve platform and an SDK that let engineers get precise, fully labeled, compliant synthetic data with a few clicks.

02 / Who it’s for

Three kinds of engineers, from clicking to coding.

Before designing, I mapped the roles involved in getting synthetic data into a computer vision pipeline. The sharpest difference between them wasn’t seniority. It was how they wanted to work.

Prefers the platform

Data scientist

Strong in Python and statistics. Wants to generate data and drop it into ML pipelines quickly.

Needs: graphical tools and ready-made functions, so they can experiment without extensive coding.

Uses both

Computer vision expert

Deep knowledge of image processing and camera models. Not yet convinced synthetic data is as good as real-world data.

Needs: the platform for basic datasets, and the SDK for fine-tuning complex scenes.

Prefers the SDK

Machine learning engineer

Works in TensorFlow and PyTorch. Cares about training and evaluation running smoothly.

Needs: direct programmatic access, for flexibility and control inside their own workflows.

What it meant

One product couldn’t serve all three. The research led to three connected tools: the self-serve platform, the SDK, and the SDK documentation. I designed the platform. I had very little to do with the SDK’s design, but I had to know how to use it and reference it in the platform, so people could move from one to the other.

03 / The journey

Two paths that meet in the same data.

People came in through the website and the platform. After becoming paying customers they got a customer-success kickoff, then split by how they liked to work.

Getting started
  1. Website
  2. Platform
  3. Becomes a paying customer
  4. Customer-success kickoff
Platform path
  1. Choose a modality
  2. Basic controls
  3. Data management
  4. Support by email
SDK path
  1. SDK and documentation
  2. Advanced controls
  3. Automation
  4. Support by Slack

The SDK path loops back: documentation sends people back to the platform, and SDK users still manage their data there.

04 / The design

A catalog you can browse, with the code one click away.

I worked closely with product, R&D and the 3D team through research, personas and journey maps, card sorting and user flows, paper wireframes, prototypes and usability checks.

04.1Home

Start something new, or check on what’s cooking

  • “What would you like to generate today?”The two main products (Humans, and Humans in Context) are the first thing you see, each with its use cases.
  • Datasets tableThe status of your latest data requests, from queued to ready to download. Generation can take days, so status is front and center.
  • DGU hours bankTells you how much data you can still generate.
  • Additional materialsGitHub, documentation, Jupyter notebooks and tutorials, for people who want to go deeper or are on their way to the SDK.
04.2Asset generation

See what you’ll get before you ask for it

  • Catalog navigationEvery attribute you can change (head, accessories, background) in one tree, with explore controls that show what each change does.
  • Query with inline guidanceHelps people narrow the search to the exact assets they want to generate.
  • Assets previewShows every asset the request will generate. The query controls how many.
  • Sample imageA zoomed-in preview of what you asked for.
  • Controls as codeThe panel shows the SDK code that produces the selection. You can tweak it, or copy it into the SDK for more flexible use. That snippet is the bridge between the two paths.
  • Request contentOne clear call to action to turn the selection into a dataset request.
05 / Design system

One system, light and dark.

I built a design system with both light and dark themes, together with the team. It made onboarding new designers easier, made it simple to roll the rebranded colors across the platform, and gave us a shared language with front-end about how components and colors should be used.

06 / Leading the team

Beyond the screens

Leading design

I led and mentored the design team, and owned the platform’s UX end to end.

Shaping the product

I defined product vision together with product, engineering, research, marketing and sales, guided by research and product data.
